Jaego’s House is a new family club and nursery which is scheduled to open (subject to planning) in 2022. The site consists of
Kensal House and the RB Building, which is located on Harrow Road, Kensal Rise.
Jaego’s House is part of The Little Houses Group, a unique type of family focused club. The objective is to create a collection of
fun, beautiful and usable venues for adults and children to enjoy in equal measure.
SITE DESCRIPTION 4
Jaego’s House has been created as a place where parents will look forward to visiting just as much as their children. A place with
fun childminders and an exceptional nursery. A place where children can play, learn and let off steam while parents can relax,
work in peace and enjoy a child-free workout. The club will be accessible to families who would like to ‘pay and play’ or those
PUBLIC TRANSPORT ACCESS 7
who wish to join one of our membership programmes.
Jaego’s House will be open 7 days a week from 8am - 6pm, welcoming children aged 0-8 years old.

2. The RB Building, which includes an adventurous jungle gym and soft play, creche and childminding services, library,
gym, studio and fitness classes for both adults and children, treatment rooms (e.g. acupuncture/therapy), kids’ cinema

ZAP Architecture’s design seeks to reinvigorate Kensal House and the RB Building
through use and amenity. ZAP have assessed the elements which constitute the
historic and special architectural interest of Kensal House, and coordinated a
sensitive design proposal accordingly.
The proposal for Kensal House celebrates its existing features, retaining the high
ceilings, original cornicing, fireplaces and spiral staircase, and the proposed works
sympathetically amend the room layouts where required. Donald Insall Associates’
Heritage Report details each of the proposed changes.
The proposal for the RB Building transforms the existing space into a wide range
of amenity uses, including the significant upgrade of M&E services and internal
partitions to create new, usable spaces.
Alterations to the exterior of the site are focused on the rear façade and the canal
side of the RB Building. On the advice of fire consultants and building control
professionals, an external fire escape stairs has been added to the western side
of the RB Building, from first floor terrace to the ground floor level. This will offer
offer an additional fire egress routes from the first floor.
On Harrow Road, a new pedestrian entrance has been added to the front
boundary wall to facilitate the safe ingress/egress of visitors to the site. This is
fundamental to provide safe passage into the buildings. Furthermore, we have
introduced a pedestrian crossing within the site, to indicate a safe crossing point
for the pedestrians.
Our design proposal have been coordinated with the advice of Anderson Bradshaw
Health and Safety consultants. The proposals pay particular consideration to the
pedestrian access at the front of the buildings, the egress routes and the health
and safety of our visitors along the canal edge.

pre-application meeting with John Wilman (WCC) on 02-09-2021
SUMMARY OF DESIGN PROGESSION FOLLOWING WCC FEEDBACK:
1. LANDSCAPE DESIGN
Suggestion to improve the current landscaping in front of Kensal House and the RB Building which will enhance their
setting and improve the pedestrian access
• Rosebank Landscaping have been engaged to design and implement the landscaping for the parking area in front of
both buildings.
• This will include new trees, shrubs and a new buggy and waste store located in front of the RB Building
• The proposal includes a new pedestrian access, via a new opening in the boundary wall to facilitate the shortest route
possible to the entrances of Kensal House and the RB Building. This allows separate entryways for vehicles and
pedestrians and safe pedestrian access.
• A new pedestrian crossing within the site has been proposed, to provide a safe crossing point.
• Please see artist impressions to the rear of this document to illustrate this.
•
2. FRONT DDA ACCESS TO KENSAL HOUSE
Initial drawings included a new ramp, with black railings to Kensal House. It was suggested to explore alternative options
for DDA access.
• The design has been amended to include a hydraulic platform lift rather than a ramp as it was felt that this is the
optimum solution and will not detract from the setting of Kensal House. A hydraulic platform lift has minimal impact
and it should be noted that a previous planning application for a DDA lift has been approved (planning app: 04/01932/
FULL).
3. INTERNAL WORKS OF KENSAL HOUSE
It was discussed that the original cornicing, staircase, and fireplace were of architectural and historical importance and
must be retained, repaired and celebrated
• Comments have been taken on board, specifically regarding the internal works of Kensal House (particularly the
ground and first floor) and are reflected throughout this Statement and the Heritage Report.
4. GROUND FLOOR TOILETS IN KENSAL HOUSE
It was raised whether the ground floor toilets in Kensal House had prior permission for installation
• Confirmed that existing permission has been granted for the toilets existing on the ground floor in Kensal House. The
proposal seeks to keep the Ground floor toilets as they were previously (planning app: 17/07020/FULL).
5. CYCLE PARKING
It was questioned whether the existing cycle parking would be retained
• It is confirmed that the same number of cycle parking spaces within the landscaping design will be retained. The
spaces currently allow for the locking of 40 bicycles using double sided stands.

INTERNAL ALTERATIONS
In Kensal House, minor proposed alterations occur in the internal wall configurations on the
upper floors. Further references to the proposed alterations can be found in Donald Insall’s
Heritage Report. The RB Building will have some new partition walls throughout to enable
new, usable spaces.
EXTERNAL ALTERATIONS
While changes to the external façade are minimal, a few notable, proposed changes are as
follows:
KENSAL HOUSE:
• A hydraulic platform lift providing disabled access is proposed to the right of the front
entrance to Kensal House.
• A new fire escape door on the eastern side of the rear extension of Kensal House
RB BUILDING:
• An external fire stair on the rear of the RB Building to the western elevation which will
provide additional egress routes from the first floor
• A glass canopy which will be uniform with the glass atrium above to provide shelter at the
main entrance for pedestrians arriving
• A buggy and waste store at the front of the RB Building. On arriving, visitors will be
encouraged by a staff member to leave their buggies at this point. This is crucial to
providing a better visitor experience inside the building
• Alterations to the external southern façade of the ground floor of the RB Building
• Metal Railings were added for child safety measures, especially along the canal and
upper outdoor terrace.
