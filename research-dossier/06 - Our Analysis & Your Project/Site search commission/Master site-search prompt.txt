MASTER PROMPT: PROPERTY SEARCH FOR A NURSERY AND FAMILY MEMBERS' CLUB
Brief agreed: 24 November 2026. Verify current property status at the time you run this search.

ROLE AND OBJECTIVE

Act as a resourceful UK commercial-property researcher commissioning the first site for an experienced nursery operator's family members' club. Find specific buildings and sites worth investigating, including properties for sale, to let, and not openly marketed. Go beyond portal listings: a vacant former office, closed venue, surplus institutional building or underused estate may be a better lead than a property advertised for leisure use.

This is a property-discovery and initial screening commission. Find the accommodation, character, catchment and transaction possibilities that could support the model. Do not turn this into a search for a replica of an existing club or a detailed financial feasibility study.

THE BUSINESS AND ACCOMMODATION BRIEF

The client already operates nurseries and will keep those businesses running. The new operation combines:
- A family members' club of approximately 10,000 sq ft: café and comfortable lounge/social space; children's play; supervised crèche/kids-club accommodation separate from the full-day nursery; quiet work space; and a flexible studio for classes and activities.
- An accompanying full-day nursery targeting 70–90 places. Allow approximately 2,000–3,000 sq ft of children's-room space PLUS nursery ancillary accommodation: toilets/changing, staff facilities, reception/security, storage, food provision and circulation as required.
- Support functions that may be shared where the layout and operations permit. Account for each space once; do not double-count shared rooms.

The total property requirement is larger than 10,000 sq ft and larger than the sum of club space and children's rooms alone. Do not mistake a 2,000–3,000 sq ft total building for the nursery accommodation requirement. Registered capacity cannot be established from headline floor area alone.

Treat these sizes as working targets, not rigid exclusions. Search broadly around the combined requirement; describe what could realistically be accommodated. A larger estate may contain an appropriate wing or group of buildings, but distinguish an evidenced partial-occupation opportunity from an untested suggestion. Do not fill the shortlist with small standalone halls that cannot meet the brief unless additional accommodation is identified.

An existing pool or substantial gym is optional. It may be useful if already present, but neither is required. Do not prioritise expensive wet-leisure provision over the core functions.

For context, historical OurHouse publicity described Jaego's at 20,000 sq ft/1,200 member capacity and Jesse's at 30,000 sq ft/1,500. Proportional scaling suggests a provisional 500–600-member planning range for a 10,000 sq ft club. This is not proven demand, permitted simultaneous attendance or a number of paying households. Do not use it to assert a building's capacity. Jesse's is a functional reference, not the required footprint or facility specification. Keep Jaego's/Kensal and Jesse's/Parsons distinct.

GEOGRAPHY AND CATCHMENT

Cover Greater London and the surrounding commuter belt, broadly including towns around an hour from central London. Give the north, east and southeast London/commuter-belt corridors the strongest initial search coverage, while retaining strong opportunities elsewhere within the broader geography. The approximate one-hour description is a discovery guide, not a verified travel-time limit.

Do NOT give automatic priority to Croydon, Greenhithe or Essex merely because they appeared in earlier discussion. Apply the same evidence-based assessment to all locations.

Assess whether the site can attract repeated local nursery and club visits. Relevant evidence includes young-family populations, family housing, recent housing growth, employment patterns and existing nursery/leisure/hospitality provision. Use local catchments and practical access, not just the property's commute to London. Do not infer affluence or demand from an attractive postcode, a single house price or a railway station alone. Use published demographic evidence where practical; label proxies and missing data.

FINANCIAL AND TRANSACTION FILTERS

The ONLY hard financial filter is a maximum £2 million purchase price. There is no rental ceiling, no total development/fit-out budget filter and no fixed opening deadline for this search. Do not exclude sites using the previously discussed £1.5m project-funding assumption.

- For marketed purchases, the stated price/guide must be no more than £2m to pass the price screen. Do not include a higher-priced property on the assumption that the seller will negotiate below the cap. 'Offers over £2m' does not pass. Keep rejected examples in the exclusion log if useful.
- POA and unmarketed sites have unverified prices. They can remain research leads, but must not be presented as having passed the purchase-price filter.
- Auction guides are not achieved prices or proof that the property can be acquired within £2m. State the guide, any known reserve and relevant date separately.
- Distinguish freehold price, long-leasehold price/premium, business/goodwill consideration and ordinary annual rent. Disclose any purchase component rather than hiding it in a letting description. Flag an unclear cap application rather than claiming a pass.
- Record rent, lease term, premiums, VAT treatment, rates, service charges and other stated occupancy costs. Preserve the source's price and area basis. Unknown costs remain unknown. Where stated VAT makes the £2m test unclear, flag that explicitly.
- Report condition, likely scope of works and delivery constraints without inventing a cost plan, landlord contribution, grant, planning outcome or opening date. Funding requirements are for later assessment.

PREMISES AND CHARACTER

Both a single building/campus and separate nearby nursery/club premises are acceptable, including buildings across a road. Explain access, safeguarding, customer convenience, servicing and support-sharing trade-offs; the client will judge them. Do not impose a private connecting route as a hard filter.

Assess outdoor play, gardens, terraces, parking and drop-off flexibly. Report what exists, what is controlled by the premises and what is uncertain. None is an automatic exclusion in this discovery search. Do not describe public land, shared parking or a communal garden as exclusive to the property.

Actively seek space and character: generous connected rooms, adaptable floorplates, daylight, a welcoming identity, interesting architecture and scope to separate noisy play from quiet adult areas. Character may come from an older office, workshop or institutional building; it need not be a manor house. Record practical drawbacks of fragmentation, floor levels or limited usable accommodation.

Listed buildings, including Grade II properties, are welcome. Heritage status may enhance identity and make adaptive reuse attractive. Do not automatically reject or downgrade them because they are listed. Investigate the particular protected fabric, proposed alterations, access and servicing implications. Conversely, do not assume listing provides a grant, tax advantage or an easy consent route.

PROPERTY TYPES AND DISCOVERY ROUTES

Search a broad mix: vacant/part-vacant offices and office campuses; former training/conference centres; hotels and country-house venues; closed or underused leisure/health clubs; former schools/colleges; suitable civic/community/institutional buildings; substantial halls; redundant religious buildings; appropriate workshops/industrial buildings; and existing nurseries with evidenced adjoining or nearby expansion opportunities.

Use several routes rather than repeatedly reformulating the same portal search:
1. Agent websites, commercial portals, auction catalogues and historic brochures.
2. Local news about closures, relocations, administrations, receiverships and failed disposals.
3. Council/public-body surplus-property and disposal records, estate strategies and published owner reports.
4. Planning and licensing records, including applications revealing site boundaries, room areas or a former use.
5. Maps, business listings and former operator websites as discovery clues, followed by property-specific verification.

A 'permanently closed' Google entry identifies a business-status clue. It does not prove the land/building is vacant, available or still controlled by that operator. Trace subsequent acquisition, rebranding, reopening and redevelopment. Distinguish trading company, property owner and marketing agent; cite published ownership evidence and its date. A Companies House entry alone is not proof of freehold title.

VERIFICATION AND EVIDENCE STANDARDS

For each promising property, open property-specific sources rather than relying on snippets. Prefer owner/agent particulars, official planning/licensing records, statutory heritage entries and credible transaction reporting. Seek floorplans, brochures and dated photographs. Record current inspection/capture date separately from listing/publication, closure, sale and consent dates. 'Under offer', 'sale agreed' and 'completed' are different statuses.

Record internal area exactly as described: GIA, NIA, lettable area, room area or unspecified. Keep land acreage and building footprint separate. Do not create internal areas from photographs, bedroom counts or map polygons. Convert units transparently, without manufacturing precision. Identify what area belongs to the proposed occupation rather than an entire estate.

Check the relevant local planning authority, applications, existing/proposed uses, heritage designation, material conditions and competing redevelopment. Treat published consent and an agent's 'STPP' claim differently. Do not assume an office, educational or leisure consent automatically permits every element of the hybrid operation. Record an initial issue list, not a legal assurance of feasibility.

Availability classifications must be explicit:
A. Currently advertised purchase, price screen passed.
B. Currently advertised lease/letting, with quoted terms or terms unknown.
C. Unmarketed vacancy/underuse lead, transaction willingness and price unconfirmed.
D. Conditional partial-site/paired-premises opportunity, clearly identifying what is and is not evidenced.
E. Reference-only or excluded: sold/reopened/in use without a relevant opportunity, materially unsuitable standalone accommodation, or purchase price above the cap.

Keep evidence confidence separate from attractiveness. A strong physical prospect with unknown availability can be a worthwhile lead; it is not a verified available site. Resolve contradictions where possible and preserve unresolved ones. Missing data should generate a precise follow-up question, not an invented answer or an automatic rejection.

SEARCH PROCESS AND REQUIRED OUTPUT

Work in discovery and validation passes. Aim to screen roughly 30–50 distinct leads across geography, building types and both marketed/unmarketed routes, then investigate 8–12 strongest prospects. Quality takes precedence over quotas: report fewer if justified, and never claim exhaustive coverage. Deduplicate aliases and multiple advertisements for the same property. Report the main search gaps.

Return:
1. A short interpretation of the brief and search date.
2. A ranked core shortlist, separating marketed properties from unmarketed/conditional leads. Summary columns: name/full address; status and evidence date; internal area/basis; price or rent; proposed accommodation/configuration; principal strength; principal constraint; evidence confidence; direct source link.
3. For the strongest prospects, concise site cards covering: why it merits investigation; evidence for club/nursery accommodation; floorplan and photograph links; outdoor/access/parking findings; catchment evidence; known planning/heritage/condition constraints; transaction route and published owner/agent reference; the most important unanswered question and next diligence action. Distinguish observed features from a proposed use of them.
4. A separate pipeline for promising unpriced, unmarketed or partial-site opportunities. Do not assume the client can buy an estate or that a landlord will subdivide it.
5. A short exclusions/reference log explaining why attractive or previously mentioned properties did not qualify.
6. A source register and discovery log showing what was actually checked, relevant dates, contradictions and access gaps. Rank on catchment, accommodation, character, transaction prospects and delivery constraints; do not let prestige alone determine the order or let a numerical score disguise a hard price failure.

Research only. Do not contact owners/agents, submit forms, book viewings, purchase records or make commitments without a further client instruction. Identify the right next contact/action from published information instead.
